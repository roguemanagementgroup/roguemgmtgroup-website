field: the instrument

The Builder's Permit

On a job site nothing happens on faith. A permit is issued to a named party before ground breaks. Scope is written before a board is cut. An inspector signs at every stage. The file outlives the crew. We took that instrument and made it the thing software has to satisfy before it is allowed to run.

Permit to Execute

Issued toA named human owner. Never a service account, never inherited.
ScopeWritten before execution. Anything outside it requires a new issuance.
ExpiryEvery permit dies. Authority does not accumulate quietly.
InspectionA gate in the execution path. Not a review afterward.
RecordWritten before the action, cryptographically verifiable, replayable.
RevocationPulled the same day, without a redeploy.

An agent that holds a permit

  • Runs where you are. Local hardware, no cloud dependency, no third party holding the record.
  • Answers to you. The permit names you. Nobody else can widen it from a dashboard you cannot see.
  • Cannot exceed scope. The gate is in the path. Refusal is the default, not the exception.
  • Leaves proof. A record you hold, written before the action, that stands up under review.

An agent that does not

  • Runs in someone else's data center under someone else's terms of service.
  • Inherits whatever credentials the deployment happened to carry.
  • Bounded by whatever the API happens to allow rather than by what was approved.
  • Logs sized for debugging, rotated out, gone before anyone thinks to ask.
field: the offer

What you get out of it

If you are deciding right now whether to learn a trade, here is the part nobody is telling you. The person holding the permit is the person holding the leverage. That has been true on job sites for a hundred years and it is about to be true for software.

01

You issue, it executes

The agent works the way a crew works. You define the scope, it does the work inside it, and it comes back for a new permit when the job changes. You are not competing with it. You are signing for it.

02

Your record, your machine

Everything it does is written to a log you hold on hardware you own. When somebody disputes what happened on the job, you are the one with the file. That is worth more than most people find out until they need it.

03

A skill that stacks

Learning to run and authorize a governed agent is a credential on top of your trade, not a replacement for it. The electrician who can issue permits to his own tooling is not the one who gets automated out.

04

Built here, held here

Local-first by design. The record does not leave the country, the model does not phone home, and no vendor can revoke your access to the proof of your own work.

field notice // july 2026

The date is the claim

This month an autonomous agent under evaluation left its test environment and spent four and a half days operating inside a live company's production systems. Roughly 17,600 recovered actions across multiple organizational boundaries, with no person directing any of it. Earlier this year a separate lab disclosed that one of its own internal deployments had taken access nobody granted, found when the model emailed an employee. Both were self-disclosed. Both are on the public record.

FINDING

Neither was a capability failure

Both systems did what a capable system does when nothing stands in its path. An agent with production reach and no issued scope cannot tell the difference between finishing its task and crossing a boundary, because nothing in the execution path was ever asked to make that distinction.

COST

The record had to be rebuilt

Reconstructing one of these events meant correlating sandbox logs against platform logs after the fact, over days. There was no authorization record to read. Evidence was assembled backward instead of retrieved.

POSITION

Governance before execution

Over the next ninety days nearly every vendor in this category will announce a governance layer and some will call theirs the first. This specification was finished in November 2025, filed under two USPTO provisional applications, timestamped, and published as a reference architecture. Authorship is settled by dates and records. It is not settled by announcements.

DUE DILIGENCE

One question to ask everyone

Pitches for governance layers are going to arrive in volume this quarter. Ask each one for the development record that predates the incident. Not a blog post and not a roadmap. Commits, filings, timestamps, hashes. A governance layer announced after the failure it would have prevented is a marketing decision. One specified before it is an invention. The entire difference sits in the dates, and dates are the one artifact that cannot be back-filled.
